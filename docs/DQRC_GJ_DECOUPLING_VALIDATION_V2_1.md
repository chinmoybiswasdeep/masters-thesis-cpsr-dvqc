## Short answers

1. **Was the near-zero V2 instantaneous NL caused by timing alignment?** Partially, but it does not rescue the conclusion. `causal_latency_profile` finds a nonzero architecture-aligned latency (`ell_0` ranges 0-8 across feature groups and candidates; at the selected candidate `ell_0=6`). Once aligned, `NL_local` at the candidate is genuinely nonzero (0.47-0.80 across discovery/confirmation points) where the old (literal, `tau=0`) `NL_instant` metric would have read ~0 -- so V2's near-zero reading WAS partly a timing-alignment artifact. But the alignment fix does not change the decoupling verdict: even measured at the correct, aligned delay, `NL_local` remains far more sensitive to the memory control `m` than to `(g,J)` (see answer 4).
2. **Does the processor contain independent temporal memory?** Yes. `X_P` alone reaches `M_bc=8.85` (vs `X_M`'s own `M_bc=1.63`), and even after leakage-safe residualization against `X_M` (train-fit-only projection), `X_P_perp` still reaches `M_bc=7.22` -- most of the processor's memory-like capacity is NOT redundant with what the memory register itself encodes. The processor is a substantial, largely-independent second memory register, not a pure nonlinear specialist.
3. **Does finite reset improve control separation?** No evidence of that in this pass -- if anything the opposite. At the candidate, reducing persistence (`K=1`: `M_bc=1.57, NL_local_bc=0.00`; `K=4`: `M_bc=2.45, NL_local_bc=0.00`) collapsed BOTH memory and local-nonlinear capacity together, while the persistent case (`K=None`: `M_bc=9.03, NL_local_bc=0.48`) had by far the most of both. A full per-K decoupling-metric re-analysis was not run (compute budget), so this is suggestive, not conclusive, but it does not support Outcome B.
4. **Does a valid interior region pass the decoupling gates?** No. A genuine 3D Sobol discovery scan found 4/16 points passing every hard validity filter (interior stencil margin, no ceiling contamination, adequate sample-to-effective-rank ratio, nontrivial local NL). At the best (multi-objective-selected, not max-NL-selected) of those 4 points, `R_memory=0.99` (need >2), `R_NL_local=0.15` (need >2, PRIMARY gate), and the output-normalized response angle is **177 degrees** -- `M` and `NL_local` respond in almost EXACTLY OPPOSITE directions in normalized control space, the opposite of the desired ~90-degree orthogonality. All three core decoupling gates fail decisively.
5. **Is the directional interface still low-back-action?** Yes, and now verified over a full trajectory, not one snapshot: `D_M(t)` mean=0.032, max=0.041, final=0.025 across 15 steps at the candidate -- consistently low throughout, comparable to or better than V1's own ~0.029 reference.
6. **What is the strongest justified claim level?** **Level 2** (memory back-action is reduced). Level 3 (local control decoupling) is explicitly NOT supported -- the three gates that test it directly all fail, decisively, at the one region searched.
